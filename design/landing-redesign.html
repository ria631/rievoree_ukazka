01 / What's in it

Four buildings. Four companies. One set of books.

Each building sits in its own s.r.o., the way a fund holds property. Reports run per company or for the whole group, and row-level security limits each user to the companies they belong to.

Seven reports

Each page answers one question that comes up at month end, for one building or all four.

01DashboardHow did last month go?Revenue, NOI, occupancy and cash, each with its 12-month line. NOI margin, WALT, DSCR and LTV against the range a lender is comfortable with.
02P&LDid we make money, and where are we off budget?Revenue to NOI to profit before tax as a bridge, for any building and period. Every line against budget, largest variance first.
03Rent rollWhich leases carry the income, and when do they end?Occupancy, passing rent against market rent, lease expiry by year, the share of rent each tenant pays, arrears.
04AR / APWho owes us, how late, and does it tie out?Aging by building, DSO and on-time rate. Open invoices reconciled to the receivables account in the ledger, to the cent.
05Budget flowDoes cash stay above the buffer?Bank actuals against the cash plan, the year-end outlook, and the lowest balance of the year against three months of debt service.
06ForecastWhat do the next 12 months look like?Built lease by lease from the rent roll. Three scenarios and five drivers to drag; the browser recalculates as you move them.
07JournalWhere does this number come from?Every posting grouped into its source document (rent invoice, supplier bill, bank line, voucher), with the debits = credits check and the trial balance.
02 / Getting data in

Nothing reaches a report until someone approves it.

Upload a CSV export from any accounting system. Each row is validated, each account's month is compared with its own history, and a manager reviews whatever was held.

  1. 1UploadA CSV from any system. Its column names are mapped after you pick the file; there is a template to download.
  2. 2ValidateEvery row is checked against the company's ruleset before anything else runs.
  3. 3ScreenEach account's month against its trailing 12 months. A large z-score holds the row.
  4. 4ReviewA manager sees the reason in plain numbers and approves or rejects.
  5. 5LoadApproval writes the journal. A trigger queues a refresh and pg_cron rebuilds the reports every minute.

Share the sheet with “anyone with the link” and paste the URL. Postgres downloads it through pg_net, parses the CSV in SQL and sends the rows down the same validation and review path as an upload.

  • Re-read every hour by pg_cron. Content it has already taken in (same SHA-256) is never imported twice.
  • Header row: Account, Date, Debit, Credit. Slovak column names, semicolons and decimal commas work too.
  • Public https only. Every redirect is re-checked; files are capped at 2 MB and 5,000 rows.
  • Systems that push post to a webhook signed with HMAC-SHA256, compared in constant time.
  • In the shared demo Sync now shows a preview and writes nothing.
